# What parents and carers told me (Oct 2026)

Six informal conversations in India: three parents (a mother in Hyderabad, a father in Hyderabad, a father in
Delhi), a grandparent in Hyderabad who looks after grandchildren, and two babysitters (Hyderabad, Bangalore). Summarised with their agreement, not quoted word for word. Names are left out. A small,
local sample: an early signal, not proof. Next step: families in the U.S., where Alexa+ is launching.

## The parents
- **Handing over today:** all three explain everything before leaving and send WhatsApp messages, and all worry the
  carer will forget something or not have understood it all.
- **While they're out:** they keep checking their phones for an update; they don't want to keep calling, but if
  they hear nothing for a long time they start to wonder whether everything is fine.
- **What they'd use:** one place for the routine, food, allergies and instructions, so the carer can check
  themselves instead of calling. None would use anything confusing or slow to set up, or that needs a lot of
  effort every time.
- **Emergencies:** they'd trust it for contacts and medical details if they're accurate and up to date, but the
  carer must still contact them directly and get medical help; it should help decide quickly, not replace calling.
- **What would stop them:** anything that isn't simple for both parents and carers (grandparents especially),
  inaccurate or hard-to-find information, or not keeping the child's details private to people they trust.

## The carers (a grandparent and two babysitters)
- **Hardest part:** remembering every instruction; every child's routine is different.
- **Why they call the parents:** food, sleep times, and what to do when the child cries or won't eat; they feel
  they call too often (especially when the parents are at work), but don't want to get it wrong either.
- **Would it help:** yes, everything in one place, checked whenever needed; it would make them feel more confident.
- **What worries them:** too many options or too much on the screen; they need it clear and quick to use while
  busy with the child, and simple enough to learn the first time.

## What I changed because of it
1. **Emergencies:** the Echo Show says straight away that the parents have been alerted, and the sitter's phone
   puts a one-tap "Call Anita" under the step (911 stays first). (All three parents: it should help the carer decide, not replace calling them.)
2. **"No news" worry:** when the sitter notes the child is asleep, the parents get one quiet "All's well at home"
   on their phones (held in busy mode). (The fathers.)
3. **A crying child:** the demo family's notes now include what Mom says to do when Maya is upset, so "she won't
   stop crying" gets the parents' own words instead of "they haven't covered this", the way a real family's would
   once they add it. (Both carers named crying as a reason they call.)
4. **Already there, confirmed by them:** answers in the parents' own words from a pasted WhatsApp message, a
   three-question check before the parents leave, large text for a grandparent, a pass that ends with the evening.
